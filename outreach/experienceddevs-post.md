**Title:** How is your team handling agent activity that shows up under engineers' own GitHub accounts?

**Body:**

At work, more and more of what shows up under my name on GitHub was actually done by an agent running with my token: review comments, issue replies, the occasional PR. My teammates' agents do the same. On GitHub, there's no way to tell which is which.

That's caused friction I didn't expect:

- **Politeness has a cost.** When a colleague leaves a review comment, I owe them a considered reply. When their agent leaves one, I might skim it, push back harder, or just fix it and move on. I can't tell which I'm looking at, so I either give everything full human attention, or I risk being curt with a person because I assumed it was their agent.
- **Approvals and merges have no text.** There's nowhere to put "an agent did this" on an approval, so "which approvals did a human actually review?" has no answer. For anyone with compliance requirements, that's a real gap.
- **The workarounds don't fit work accounts.** Signature lines and "generated by" footers depend on every tool and every person remembering, and every team invents its own format. Separate bot accounts or GitHub Apps aren't really an option when accounts are provisioned through SSO and every seat is a person.

What I'd want is boring: a standard way for an agent to mark its own actions (a setting on the token, say), and GitHub showing that next to the author's name. Not AI detection, and not a restriction. Just a way for well-behaved tools to be honest about what they did.

I made a small mockup to illustrate the problem, if it helps: https://mweberxyz.github.io/a-simple-request/

What I'm curious about:

- Does your team have norms for agent-authored comments, reviews, or PRs? Are they written down anywhere?
- Has anyone seen a disclosure convention actually stick across a team or org?
- Do you let agents approve or merge at all? If so, how do you keep track?
- Or is the right answer that anything under your name is yours, full stop, and how it got written doesn't matter?
